# Disce — Making Expertise Visible

**Our Vision, Strategy & Market Positioning**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Disce is an AI-powered learning system for advanced professional German (B2–C2), built for a specific kind of person: the internationally trained professional whose expertise is real — validated, experienced, often exceptional — but whose competence becomes invisible at the decisive moment because it must be performed in German.

The physician preparing for the medical licensing examination. The nurse navigating the professional recognition procedure. The consultant, engineer, or finance professional who delivers excellent work but hits a career ceiling the day meetings switch to German. All of them share one experience:

> **"I am competent — but I cannot show it when it matters."**

Disce exists to remove that barrier. We are not a language learning app. We are a **professional performance layer**: a system that starts from your concrete goal — passing the exam, completing recognition, winning the promotion — and builds a measurable, personalized bridge to it. Our approach combines proven learning science, state-of-the-art speech and language AI, and a deep library of realistic, DACH-specific professional scenarios that no other platform offers.

Strategically, Disce occupies a unique position at the intersection of three dimensions: **maximum German specialization**, **maximum AI sophistication**, and **maximum outcome orientation**. Our competitive analysis shows that no other player in the market currently occupies this space. We intend to define it — deeply, durably, and in partnership with the institutions that shape professional life in Germany.

## 2. Our Vision & Mission

### Vision

**Disce makes the expertise of international professionals visible — by making them effective in exactly the German moments that decide their professional future.**

### Mission

To develop and scale an AI-driven learning architecture that produces situational, register-aware, professional language performance — and to embed it into the regulated and career-relevant workflows where that performance actually counts.

### Why this matters now

We believe language is the key to self-determination. For highly qualified professionals, it is the difference between being tolerated and being heard — between holding a qualification and being able to practice it.

There is also a broader shift underway. As AI makes routine output abundant, accountable human judgment becomes scarce. Translation tools can carry a message across a language barrier — but they cannot carry responsibility. A physician must stand behind their own words in a patient conversation; a professional must own their contribution in a negotiation. **Disce develops the person who must stand behind what they say.** We are not betting against better translation. We are betting on people.

## 3. The Disce Philosophy: Our Core Principles

### Principle 1 — We think backwards from the goal

Most language products start from the language: vocabulary lists, grammar units, streak counters. Disce starts from the outcome. You define your concrete objective — passing the *Fachsprachprüfung*, completing professional recognition, leading the client meeting, earning tenure — and the system builds a personalized roadmap backwards from that goal. Certificates are milestones along the way, not the destination. Progress is measured in real-world readiness, not in points or badges.

### Principle 2 — Language is register, not just grammar

The single most common failure point for advanced German learners is not grammar. It is **register**: the ability to move fluidly between professional terminology (*Fachsprache*) and everyday language (*Umgangssprache*), to explain a diagnosis to a patient in plain, calm words, to document a case in the exact structured format an examiner expects, to present to a board with the right level of formality. Disce's feedback system is built specifically around this — analyzing every response against two questions: *Did the response fulfill the communicative task?* And: *What language evidence explains its quality?* Good language does not rescue a missed task.

### Principle 3 — Vertical depth over horizontal breadth

The dominant playbook in consumer software is to go broad: more languages, more users, more features. We have chosen the opposite. Like the most durable deep-technology companies of our era, we believe **the architecture is the moat** — and that architecture is built by fitting a product so precisely to one demanding context that no generalist platform can replicate it without rebuilding from the foundation.

A mass-market app serves a beginner's journey. A certification institute serves a general curriculum. Neither can serve the internationally trained professional who is *not a language beginner at all* — who already possesses deep competence and needs to perform it in a new linguistic register, under pressure, in a regulated context. Serving that person is not a market segment for us. It is our entire structural orientation.

We deliberately focus on what practitioners in vertical AI call the **final one or two percent of a use case**: the nuance of a medical licensing conversation, the register of German professional documentation, the cultural texture of a German workplace. These are precisely the depths that are too niche for large horizontal platforms to justify — and exactly where real professional value lives.

### Principle 4 — Conviction on the science, iteration on the experience

Our learning architecture is not a collection of features. It is a **causal theory** — grounded in decades of second-language acquisition research, cognitive science, and established frameworks for measuring spoken language performance (complexity, accuracy, and fluency). We hold ourselves to a high bar before changing that foundation: only robust outcome evidence moves the science, never passing preference trends.

This discipline is deliberate, and it protects the one thing our users rely on us for. Decades of learning science show that effective learning is often cognitively demanding: methods that build durable, transferable competence frequently feel harder and less immediately rewarding than the drills and flashcards users have seen elsewhere. If a learning system is guided only by what users say feels pleasant, it will drift toward what feels easy — and away from what works. We will not make that trade. When our users tell us an interface is confusing, a scenario feels irrelevant, or an onboarding step creates unnecessary friction, we iterate immediately and gratefully. When they ask us to make the learning itself shallower, we point to the evidence — and to their own exam dates and career milestones.

The distinction maps to a simple internal rule we hold to: **defend the causal theory; iterate the delivery mechanism.**

### Principle 5 — Evidence over engagement

We do not optimize for streaks, gamification, or time-in-app. We optimize for measurable performance change in the situations that matter. Our product development runs on disciplined build–deploy–measure–learn cycles: we deploy into the field, measure what users can actually do, honestly document what worked and what did not, and rebuild what needs rebuilding.

We distinguish rigorously between two kinds of questions: *"Is our architecture working?"* — answered only by objective outcome checkpoints at defined milestones — and *"Do users find the experience clear, relevant, and manageable?"* — answered continuously by user feedback and usage behavior. Both matter enormously. Conflating them is how well-founded learning products lose their competitive core.

We apply the same honesty externally that we apply internally. Where our evidence is complete, we say so; where questions remain open, we say that too. **We are building the evidence system that can produce certainty — rather than claiming certainty we do not yet have.** We believe this discipline is the only durable basis for the trust of learners, employers, and institutions.

### Principle 6 — Privacy and compliance as product features, not obligations

Disce is engineered in Europe, for Europe's most demanding professional contexts. Voice data, learning diagnostics, and institutional reporting are handled with GDPR-native, EU-hosted architecture from the ground up. Learner interaction data is never exposed to third-party commercial model training pipelines — a deliberate governance commitment that protects our users' professional data and, by extension, their trust.

For hospitals, employers, universities, and public institutions, this is not a checkbox — it is a prerequisite for adoption, and a structural advantage that generic, non-European platforms struggle to match. We treat data responsibility as a core product property, never as an afterthought.

### Principle 7 — Trust is built through institutions

In regulated professional markets, trust flows through institutions: medical associations, hospital networks, employers, universities, professional bodies. We deliberately seek these partnerships — not merely as distribution channels, but as deep collaborations in which Disce becomes part of how an institution actually works: onboarding pathways, recognition procedures, professional development programs. Trust, once earned inside a regulated institution, cannot be bought. It can only be built — and it compounds.

## 4. Strategic Market Positioning

### A unique position in the market

To understand where Disce stands, map the market on three dimensions: **specialization** (how deeply a product targets advanced, DACH-specific professional German), **AI sophistication** (from traditional courses to voice-first, real-time AI coaching), and **outcome orientation** (from generic practice to measurable professional performance).

Across all three lenses, the same picture emerges:

- **Mass-market platforms** (the Duolingos and Babbels of the world) have scale and AI, but they serve beginners across dozens of languages — no DACH depth, no B2–C2 focus, no professional outcomes.
- **Established German institutions** (such as the Goethe-Institut) offer deep German and credibility, but traditional, non-AI delivery that cannot provide real-time, personalized performance coaching.
- **AI-native startups** offer impressive spoken-language technology, but as horizontal players across many languages — without the German professional depth, and without the regulatory context that German professional life demands.

**The intersection of all three dimensions — deep German specialization, voice-first AI, and professional outcome orientation — is unoccupied. Disce is there.**

This is not a marginal gap. It is a structural position, protected by barriers on every side: technology leaders have no incentive to narrow to one language and one professional context; language specialists lack the AI architecture; and any new entrant would need to build not just features, but an entire integrated system — diagnostic science, scenario depth, cultural specificity, and institutional trust — from scratch.

### Our value proposition

**For professionals:** You already have the expertise. Disce gives you the German that makes it visible — through adaptive diagnostics that find your exact performance gaps, realistic simulations of the situations you face, phoneme-accurate spoken feedback, register coaching that fixes the *causes* of miscommunication rather than the symptoms, and progress you can actually see. All in focused sessions designed to fit demanding schedules.

**For institutions and employers:** Disce turns language from an unmanaged risk into a measurable asset. Whether you are a hospital onboarding internationally trained physicians, an employer navigating recognition procedures, or a company developing international talent — Disce provides auditable progress data, faster time-to-effectiveness, and a defensible answer to the question every institution faces: *does the investment work?* Where the buyer cares about outcomes and the user cares about the daily experience, our architectural anchor is the outcome — with user experience as the delivery mechanism that makes it achievable.

## 5. Our Strategy in Action

### The beachhead principle: win completely before expanding

Our growth follows a deliberate, proven logic: **dominate one sharply-defined market before opening the next.** Rather than spreading thin across the entire "learn German" market, we concentrate relentlessly on the segments where the stakes are highest, the need is most acute, and the fit with our architecture is strongest. Depth in one segment generates the outcome data, the reference cases, and the institutional trust that make the next segment faster to win. Expanding too early — before the system's value is proven in context — is the classic failure mode of promising young companies, and we have built our sequencing to avoid it.

### The people we serve

Our research across the professional landscape identified a set of audiences that appear different on the surface but share one architectural truth: **their barrier is not knowledge, but the situational performance of it in German.**

| Audience | The decisive moment | What success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Internationally trained physicians** | The *Fachsprachprüfung* — the structured medical language examination that stands between a fully qualified doctor and the right to practice in Germany | Performing clinical communication — patient conversation, documentation, case handover — with the register, structure, and calm the exam demands |
| **International nursing professionals** | The recognition procedure — a long, demanding pipeline in which language is both a formal requirement and a daily operational necessity | Full professional recognition, shorter timelines, confidence in shift handovers and patient communication |
| **White-collar professionals** in finance, consulting, engineering, pharma, legal, and the German Mittelstand | Not one exam, but a recurring cluster: the meeting that switches to German, the performance review, the promotion, the client-facing role | Breaking through the B2 ceiling — presenting, negotiating, leading, and belonging at full professional level |
| **International academics** | The transition from researcher to permanent faculty, where teaching, governance, and committee work move into German | Navigating academic self-governance and appointment processes at full professional standing |

These audiences share something deeper than a market label. The psychological core — *professional visibility through language* — is identical. That is why one learning architecture can serve all of them: **the engine is the same; the professional context is the configuration.**

### One engine, many contexts

Disce's learning engine — situational, task-based learning; adaptive progression; register-aware feedback; high-stakes scenario simulation — is segment-agnostic. What changes between a physician, a nurse, a consultant, and an academic is the scenario library, the professional vocabulary, and the entry point (exam date, recognition stage, next career step). This is what makes Disce scalable without dilution: **one business model, one scientific core, many professional worlds.**

### From individual learners to institutional partnerships

Our go-to-market follows a natural sequence that we describe as **B2C → B2C2B → B2B**:

1. **Individual professionals** experience the product directly and generate the most important asset we have: real outcome data — exam results, recognition timelines, promotions, testimonials.
2. **Those users become champions inside their organizations** — the physician in a hospital, the hire in a corporate team — creating organic, credibility-rich demand from within.
3. **Institutions** — hospital networks, employers, universities, professional bodies — adopt Disce through structured pilots with clearly defined success metrics, which mature into long-term partnerships with deep workflow integration.

For institutional buyers, the logic is straightforward: language failure carries real costs — delayed recognition, prolonged onboarding, liability risk, turnover. Disce's value is measurable in exactly those terms, and our partnerships are built around demonstrating it.

### Why speed matters — and why durability matters more

We are candid that our window of opportunity is time-limited: competitive windows in AI close faster than in traditional software. But the race is not won by demo speed — it is won by depth. A competitor can add a German option in months; replicating professional diagnostic depth, scenario libraries, and institutional relationships takes years, and catching up later means overcoming *compounding* disadvantages, not just matching current features.

Our strategy is therefore focused on converting early-mover advantage into durable position: deepening the feedback loops that make our diagnostic system more precise with every session, accumulating outcome evidence, and embedding into institutional workflows *before* the space becomes crowded. We aim to be the **last mover** in our category — the company that defined the standard everyone else must measure against.

Situational. Adaptive. Professional.
